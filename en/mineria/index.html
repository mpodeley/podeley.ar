Four cases, all live.

Know who holds the ground before making a move

Argentina has no national mining cadastre: each province publishes its own in a different format, and several publish nothing at all. This unifies six into one map — 19,671 tenements, 22 million hectares — with the holder, the mineral and the file number behind every polygon. San Juan has 66.4% of its territory under mining title; Jujuy, 66.2%.

Rank drill targets before paying for the field campaign

Targets ranked from satellite spectrometry (ASTER, EMIT), blind-validated by re-finding known mines — Pirquitas, Chinchillas. Paper available, plus an earlier San Juan case.

Audit how much rock a mine moved, from the outside

Excavated volume estimated by differencing satellite elevation models: ≈285 Mm³ at Veladero; a Chinese coal mine checked against reported figures. Useful where operator data is opaque.

Early warning of how the salar responds to pumping

Satellite radar over pumped lithium salars (Hombre Muerto): deformation is there, but small — near the noise floor. Published anyway.

Cybersecurity · Energy and critical infrastructure · Argentina

The sector learns what happened to it from whoever attacked it

Between 2020 and 2026, fourteen cyber incidents were documented across Argentine energy and critical infrastructure. Seven became known because the attacker posted the victim on its leak site. There is no reporting duty and no place where companies share what they see. The first three pieces measure the exposure with open sources, without scanning anyone; the fourth proposes who sits at the table.

What breaks

In the Argentine cases, nomination and dispatch went down

In the two incidents where the affected system is known, the target was the layer that turns contracts into scheduled flow. The gas kept moving. Scheduling moved to phone and spreadsheet, balances were rebuilt afterward and imbalance penalties ended up in dispute. On the org chart that layer is administrative, so it falls outside the OT perimeter that industrial security is built around.

The State runs a critical-infrastructure cybersecurity program financed by the Inter-American Development Bank, with a 9% baseline measured in 2021 and a 50% target for 2028. It includes an information-sharing product with the private sector, budgeted and unexecuted. The companies that would need to sit at the table have been co-owners of CAMMESA, the power dispatch operator, through their associations since 1992.

Meanwhile each operator faces the same groups on its own, without knowing what worked for the last one.

Every new row and every correction to the register goes out through the Atom feed. If an incident is missing or something is wrong, emailing with the source is enough to get it corrected.

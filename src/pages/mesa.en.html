Sector table · Cybersecurity · Argentine energy

Who would need to sit at the table, and in what order

A map of the institutions Argentine energy companies would need in order to share threat information: what each can contribute and what it needs to join. A critical-infrastructure working group put it together as the basis for a first conversation with IAPG and ARPEL.

← Cybersecurity in energy November 5, 2026 The actors ↓ The plan ↓ The conversation ↓

Starting point

The State is about to see the sector's exposure, with nobody to talk to

The National Cybersecurity Center is buying a platform that scans the entire Argentine IP space every 12 hours. Much of what it finds will belong to private energy operators, over which the Center has no mandate. In September its then director said the relationship with private companies is one of collaboration and that they are seeking agreements with the regulators.

On the private side there is no entity to receive those findings and no rules for handling them. Over six years, fourteen incidents became known in the sector, seven only because the attacker posted them. This page proposes how to build the counterpart. The model and its costs are in the memo.

The actors

Six groups, from those who convene to those who operate

What each can contribute comes from its public role or what it publishes. What it needs to join is our own reading, to be tested in the conversations.

The plan

The host and the anchors first, then the State

If the State convenes before operators are committed, the table fills with officials and vendors. Asking the State questions can start now, because it commits no one.

0

Conversations, now

The register, the attack-surface map and the memo are already published. Next come eight conversations with trade bodies and operators, starting with IAPG and ARPEL, and an inquiry to the IDB about the exchange output. If the eight produce neither a host nor a state signature, the problem is demand, and it is better to stop.

1

The table

A host (the IAPG committee or an electricity association), three or four anchor operators, a confidentiality agreement and a cooperation consortium contract, which creates no legal entity. Exchange of alerts and indicators; no sensors.

2

The entity

A nonprofit association with tiered dues from day one and operations contracted out. The minimum scenario costs about USD 270,000 a year, and four anchors cover it.

3

Scale and rules

Sensors where someone asks for them, water and transport, a yearly sector exercise and the bill. With two years of its own data, the table comes to the rule-making debate as a technical source.

The conversation

What to bring to IAPG and ARPEL, and what to ask them

IAPG can host the national table and ARPEL already runs a regional best-practice exchange. The conversation is about whether they are interested and on what terms.

Four things go to the meeting: the incident register, the attack-surface map, the memo with costs and drafts, and this page.

  1. Can the IAPG Cybersecurity Committee host an exchange table among operators, under a confidentiality agreement?
  2. What information-handling rules would an operator's legal team need before approving participation?
  3. Which operators would sit down in a first round?
  4. How is the work split with the ARPEL committee: best practices at regional scale, alerts and incidents at national scale?
  5. Who talks to the National Cybersecurity Center and the Energy Secretariat, and when?

The ask is one working meeting of the committee with two or three operators. There is no ask for funding, posts or anyone's incident data.

Limits

What this map does not say

It does not say what any of the institutions named thinks: none was consulted for this page. The column on what each needs is a hypothesis to test in the conversations.

It names no operators. The tables go by subsector on purpose, so the map can be used to convene without singling anyone out.

What it says about the IAPG Cybersecurity Committee and the ARPEL committee comes from what they publish as of November 5, 2026. There may be activity that is not public.

Building got cheap. Choosing did not

Current models will put an internal tool together in days. What never got cheaper is knowing which one is worth building, and that comes in three sizes.

The involvement

One decision, one project, or the whole role

It almost always starts small. What sets the level is how often the decisions come up, not how big the company is.

Days

One-off consult

One decision on the table and the question that moves it: a second opinion on a vendor's proposal, a data room review, an assumption worth checking before you sign. It closes in a meeting or two, on published data plus whatever your side brings.

Weeks

Project

Fixed scope, a deliverable and a handover: a diagnosis with its sources and assumptions in the open, a model or dashboard your team runs on its own, or a full build with whatever counterparties it takes. The four steps below describe this level.

Ongoing

Standing engagement

When what to build, what to buy and what to hand to a model stop being an assignment and start getting decided every week, what is missing is a role rather than a deliverable: technology direction at the fraction the organization actually uses.

The process

Four steps

The same ones for a small diagnostic or a full project. What changes is how long each takes.

1

The call

Twenty minutes: which decisions lie ahead, and which question moves each one. The published cases are the inspiration: that kind of thing, with your operation's own data inside.

2

The proposal

Within that same week: scope, timeline and deliverables in writing. When the case calls for it, it ships with a mockup or demo of the solution, so the discussion runs on something concrete instead of a document.

3

The scope

Before anything starts we talk scope alternatives and roadmap. More depth, more iterations or extra specialists cost more; if a simple, approximate solution is enough to decide, it costs you less. That trade-off is yours to pick, with the options on the table.

4

The delivery

Built, maintained and handed over: the tool ends up running wherever you decide, with your team trained, or an outsourced one if you don't have it yet. No permanent dependency on the consultant.

If the call shows there is no way to help, that gets said on the spot.

The deliverable

What stays behind

Whatever the decision calls for: a diagnosis with sources and assumptions in the open, a model or dashboard your team runs on its own, or a full project, with the team and the counterparties it takes. The proposal pins it down in writing: what gets delivered, when, and what stays on your side at the end.

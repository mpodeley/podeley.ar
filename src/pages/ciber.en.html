Cybersecurity in Argentine energy

Between 2020 and 2026, fourteen cyber incidents were documented across Argentine energy and critical infrastructure. Seven became known because the attacker posted the victim on its leak site. There is still no place where companies share what they see. These four pieces, built from open sources without scanning anyone, are a basis for setting one up.

  • The register

    The known incidents, one per row: date, subsector, actor and source, with what the organization declared and what the attacker claimed in separate columns. Plus cards on the groups, the international context and the legal framework.

  • The map

    The gas and power networks, what depends on each node, the sector's internet footprint in aggregate and the actors that govern it.

What breaks

Which systems went down

In the two incidents where the affected system is known, the target was the layer that turns contracts into scheduled flow. The gas kept moving. Scheduling moved to phone and spreadsheet, balances were rebuilt afterward and imbalance penalties ended up in dispute. On the org chart that layer counts as administrative, between IT and OT.

The State runs a critical-infrastructure cybersecurity program financed by the Inter-American Development Bank, with a 9% baseline measured in 2021 and a 50% target for 2028. It includes an information-sharing product with the private sector, still pending. The companies a table would convene have worked together in CAMMESA, the power dispatch operator, through their associations since 1992.

Every new row and every correction to the register goes out through the Atom feed. If an incident is missing or something is wrong, emailing with the source is enough to get it corrected.
